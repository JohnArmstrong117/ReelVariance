BEYOND THE AVERAGE · DEMO DATA

How much do viewers disagree?

Controversy Score — The Controversy Score measures how spread out viewers’ ratings are. When most viewers give a movie similar ratings, its score is low. When ratings range from “hated it” to “loved it,” its score can be much higher.

How it works. We use a statistic called variance to measure how far ratings tend to fall from a movie’s average rating. To score a genre, we average its movies’ scores, giving each movie equal weight.

Chart methods & exact values

Demo ratings and simulated dates through 2023. Bars and trend lines give each film equal weight; multi-genre films appear in each listed genre. Time periods include only films with at least two ratings in that period, so the set of contributing films can change. Calendar periods may be partial. Trends compare rating cohorts, not changes in the same people.

Violins pool ratings per genre and smooth them with a Gaussian kernel (bandwidth 0.25 stars, reflected at 0.5 and 5). All violins use a shared width scale. Smoothing is illustrative: use the exact counts below before interpreting apparent peaks.

A LITTLE CONTEXT

What does disagreement look like?

01

Average = overall opinion

The mean adds all star ratings and divides by the number of ratings. It hides how those ratings are distributed.

02

Variance = spread of opinion

We use sample variance: sum of squared distances from the mean, divided by n − 1. Higher values indicate a wider spread, not necessarily two opposing camps. Units are stars squared.

03

Distribution = the fuller picture

Check the histogram. A single cluster and two opposing clusters can tell very different stories. Sample size matters; a minimum-count filter is a convenience, not a confidence guarantee.

Prototype scope & data

All 12 films use synthetic rating histograms on the MovieLens 0.5–5.0 star scale. The Overview time chart uses deterministic simulated yearly partitions, ending in 2023; they sum exactly to each film’s full histogram. They do not represent observed historical trends. The Overview bars average film-level means and variances equally; violin plots pool rating counts. The time chart averages within-film variance in each period. Multi-genre films belong to every listed genre. Titles are used only to make the interaction recognizable. Counts, averages and variances are computed from those demo histograms. No real review text, critic scores or sentiment model is included.

The intended production source is MovieLens 32M. MovieLens user IDs are anonymous; its ratings do not identify professional critics. Ratings can reveal disagreement, but cannot establish why people disagree. IMDb review analysis would be a separate future integration. The IMDb Spoiler Dataset provides review text and movie metadata; it is not yet used in these charts.
